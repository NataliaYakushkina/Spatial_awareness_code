# Farmhouse in Provence

## Metadata

- **Title:** Farmhouse in Provence (Mas de Provence) / also known as Entrance Gate to a Farm with Haystacks
- **Artist:** Vincent van Gogh (1853–1890)
- **Year:** June 1888
- **Medium:** Oil on canvas
- **Dimensions:** 46.1 × 60.9 cm (18.1 × 24 in)
- **Catalogue raisonné:** F 565, JH 1443
- **Location:** National Gallery of Art, Washington, D.C. (Ailsa Mellon Bruce Collection, 1970)
- **Provenance:** Theo van Gogh, 1888; Jo van Gogh-Bonger, 1891; sold through Julien Tanguy, Paris, to Willy Gretor, 1891; Maria Slavona; Otto Ackermann; Gaston Bernheim de Villers, by 1919; Capt. Edward Molyneux; Ailsa Mellon Bruce, 1955; bequeathed to National Gallery of Art, 1970
- **Period:** Arles
- **Genre:** Landscape painting

## Subject

A Provençal farmhouse seen through its entrance gate, with golden haystacks in the foreground courtyard. The stone buildings with terracotta roofs are nestled among lush vegetation. A lone figure walks in the field beyond. The sky above is animated with pink-tinted clouds set against turquoise blue. The composition is framed by the gate and the surrounding greenery, drawing the eye into the warm, sun-drenched courtyard and beyond to the open landscape.

## Visual Description

- **Palette:** Three pairs of complementary colours animate the scene: orange-gold haystacks against the blue sky, red-green vegetation, and pink clouds against turquoise. The colour contrast brings an extraordinary intensity and vibrancy to the modest rural subject.
- **Brushwork:** Vigorous, directional strokes following the forms of haystacks, vegetation, and sky. The paint application is energetic but controlled, with visible individual brushstrokes that create texture throughout.
- **Composition:** The farmhouse gate creates a natural frame, leading the eye from the foreground haystacks through the courtyard to the distant landscape. The composition balances solidity (architecture, haystacks) with openness (sky, distant field).

## Context

Van Gogh arrived in Arles in February 1888, seeking the brilliant light and vivid colours of Provence. He described Arles as "the Japan of the South," a land where sunlight simplified forms into the flat patterns he admired in Japanese woodblock prints. In approximately 15 months — 444 days — he produced over 200 paintings, 100 drawings, and 200 letters.

Farmhouse in Provence was painted in June 1888, at the height of his Arles period. Van Gogh was 35 and producing some of his finest work. His use of complementary colours was deeply informed by Michel Eugène Chevreul's colour theories, which he had studied in Nuenen, and by Adolphe Monticelli's bold colour and heavy paint application, which he had encountered through Theo in Paris. It was partly Monticelli, a native of Marseilles, who inspired Van Gogh's move south.

## Artistic Intent

- **Apply complementary colour theory systematically.** The three pairs of complementary colours (orange/blue, red/green, pink/turquoise) are a conscious demonstration of Chevreul's principles.
- **Capture Provençal light.** Van Gogh sought a brilliant light that would "wash out detail and simplify forms" into the flat patterns he admired in Japanese art.
- **Render rural life with dignity.** The simple farmhouse is presented with the same care and intensity Van Gogh brought to all his subjects.

## Stylistic Significance

- **Exemplary Arles-period landscape.** The painting demonstrates the full flowering of Van Gogh's colour theory at Arles — bold complementary contrasts applied to rural Provençal subjects.
- **Japanese-Provençal synthesis.** The flattened forms and intense colours represent Van Gogh's distinctive merger of Japanese aesthetic principles with European oil technique in the southern French landscape.
- **Transitional technique.** The balance between careful colour planning and spontaneous brushwork illustrates the moment when Van Gogh's intellectual engagement with colour theory and his emotional expressiveness achieved equilibrium.
