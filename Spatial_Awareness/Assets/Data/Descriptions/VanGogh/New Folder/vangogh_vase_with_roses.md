# Roses (Vase with Roses)

## Metadata

- **Title:** Roses (Rozen)
- **Artist:** Vincent van Gogh (1853–1890)
- **Year:** May 1890
- **Medium:** Oil on canvas
- **Dimensions:** 93 × 74 cm (36.6 × 29.1 in)
- **Catalogue raisonné:** F 682, JH 1979
- **Location:** The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York (The Walter H. and Leonore Annenberg Collection, Gift of Walter H. and Leonore Annenberg, 1993, Bequest of Walter H. Annenberg, 2002. Inv. no. 1993.400.5)
- **Provenance:** With the artist's mother, Anna van Gogh-Carbentus, until 1907; estate sold to Paul Cassirer, Berlin, 1908; Margarete Oppenheim; Georg Simon Hirschland, Essen (seized by Nazis 1939; restituted to heirs, 1950); Albert D. and Mary Lasker, New York; Walter H. and Leonore Annenberg; Metropolitan Museum of Art
- **Period:** Saint-Rémy-de-Provence
- **Genre:** Still life / Flower painting

## Subject

A luxuriant bouquet of roses overflowing from a green vase, set against a light yellow-green background. The flowers — now appearing white but originally painted in pink — fill the upper two-thirds of the canvas in an exuberant, tumbling mass of curved petals and dark green leaves. The vase sits on a surface rendered with horizontal strokes. Traces of pink remain along the tabletop and some petals, offering a faint reminder of the formerly more vivid colour. The composition is vertical, with the bouquet rising and spreading from the central vase.

## Visual Description

- **Palette:** Currently dominated by whites, cream, and light greens. Originally, the roses were pink and the intended effect was a contrast of complementary colours — pink against green. The fugitive red lake pigment has faded over 130+ years, transforming the colour scheme from warm pink-green contrast to cool white-green harmony.
- **Brushwork:** Extremely thick, heavily loaded paint. The impasto is so substantial that the canvas required weeks to dry and had to be left behind when Van Gogh departed Saint-Rémy on May 16, 1890. The paint is applied in undulating, diagonal strokes that animate the surface and follow the furled forms of petals and leaves.
- **Composition:** Vertical format. The bouquet is centred in the vase, with the horizon line matching the companion paintings in the series (Van Gogh Museum Irises, Met Irises, NGA Roses). The overall composition is generous and expansive — the flowers seem to overflow the vase and press against the edges of the canvas.

## Context

Roses is one of four monumental flower still lifes that Van Gogh painted in May 1890 on the eve of his departure from the Saint-Rémy asylum — two of irises and two of roses, in contrasting formats and colour schemes. He cut the four canvases from the same roll and designed them with a common horizon line, conceiving them as a unified series comparable to his Sunflower decorations from Arles.

Van Gogh described working on the series in letters to Theo dated May 11 and 13, 1890, mentioning "a canvas of pink roses against a yellow-green background in a green vase." He sought to achieve a "calm, unremitting ardor" in his "last touch of the brush" before leaving Provence.

The painting was owned by Van Gogh's mother until her death in 1907. As early as that year, the once-pink roses were described as "white" by a visitor who saw the painting in her home, documenting how rapidly the pigment had faded. The painting's provenance includes a dark chapter: it was seized by the Nazis from Georg Simon Hirschland in 1939 and only restituted to his heirs in 1950.

## Artistic Intent

- **Create a complementary colour study.** The original pink-against-green scheme was a deliberate exploration of complementary colour contrast — a subject that fascinated Van Gogh throughout his career.
- **Complete a decorative ensemble.** The four flower paintings were conceived as a unified group, bringing Van Gogh's ambition for monumental flower decorations to its fullest expression.
- **Achieve serene intensity.** Van Gogh described his goal as "calm, unremitting ardor" — the union of passionate engagement with contemplative serenity.
- **Celebrate the fullness of life.** Van Gogh saw blossoming plants as celebrations of birth and renewal. The exuberant bouquet, in the glory of full bloom, embodies this life-affirming vision.

## Stylistic Significance

- **Part of Van Gogh's final flower series.** The four Saint-Rémy still lifes represent the culmination of his decorative flower painting, surpassing even the Sunflowers in ambition and chromatic sophistication.
- **Case study in pigment degradation.** The fading of the pink roses to white is one of the most dramatic and well-documented examples of colour change in art history, fundamentally altering the viewer's experience of the painting.
- **Among his largest and most beautiful still lifes.** At 93 × 74 cm, Roses is one of Van Gogh's most monumental flower paintings — a commanding presence on the museum wall.
- **Restitution history.** The painting's Nazi seizure and postwar restitution adds an important art-historical dimension to its provenance.
