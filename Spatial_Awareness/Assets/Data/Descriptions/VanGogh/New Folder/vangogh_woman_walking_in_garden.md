# A Woman Walking in a Garden

## Metadata

- **Title:** A Woman Walking in a Garden (Femme dans un Jardin)
- **Artist:** Vincent van Gogh (1853–1890)
- **Year:** June–July 1887
- **Medium:** Oil on canvas
- **Dimensions:** 48 × 60 cm (18.9 × 23.6 in)
- **Catalogue raisonné:** F 368, JH 1262
- **Location:** Private collection (sold Sotheby's London, 2005)
- **Provenance:** Cornelis Hoogendijk; Mr. and Mrs. Gilli, Paris; sold Sotheby's London to anonymous buyer, 2005; private collection. Exhibited at Art Institute of Chicago, 2023.
- **Period:** Paris
- **Genre:** Landscape painting

## Subject

An unidentified woman walks through a lush, verdant garden in the northern suburbs of Paris, near Asnières or Clichy. She moves along a path amid a profusion of red and yellow flowers, surrounded by tall trees and dense greenery. In the background, a building is partially concealed behind a thick row of trees. A distinctive red paint border runs along the edges of the canvas, framing the scene and connecting it to its companion works in the Riverbank in Clichy Triptych — Fishing in Spring (Art Institute of Chicago) and River Bank in Springtime (Dallas Museum of Art).

## Visual Description

- **Palette:** Vibrant greens dominate — the lush foliage of suburban gardens in high summer. Dots and dashes of red and yellow mark the flowers. The woman's figure provides a darker accent within the bright landscape. The red painted border frames the composition. The palette is significantly brighter than Van Gogh's earlier Dutch period work.
- **Brushwork:** Short, distinct, directional strokes influenced by Neo-Impressionism. The technique is more spontaneous and varied than strict pointillism — Van Gogh adapts the method to capture the textures of foliage, flowers, and pathway with individual touches of colour.
- **Composition:** The woman is placed within a deep, receding garden space, with the path creating a diagonal leading into the background. The trees and foliage frame and partially enclose the scene.

## Context

Van Gogh created this painting during the summer of 1887, when he was making almost daily trips from Paris to the suburban areas of Asnières, Clichy, and their surroundings. Research has established that he made this commute nearly every day for three months between May and July 1887, producing approximately 40 paintings in the area. He was inspired to venture to the suburbs after viewing Paul Signac's Ponton de la Félicité in 1887.

The painting is part of the Riverbank in Clichy Triptych, along with Fishing in Spring and River Bank in Springtime. All three share the distinctive red border marking them as a group. The identity of the woman remains unknown — Van Gogh's letters from this period are fewer than from other times, since he was living with Theo and had no need to write.

Van Gogh wrote to his sister Willemien in October 1887: "When I painted the landscape at Asnières this summer I saw more colour in it than before."

## Artistic Intent

- **Experiment with Impressionist colour.** The painting marks Van Gogh's dramatic shift from his dark Dutch palette to the vibrant colours of Impressionism and Neo-Impressionism.
- **Explore the suburban landscape.** The northern suburbs of Paris provided subject matter that combined natural beauty with modern life.
- **Create a triptych ensemble.** The red border connects this painting to its companions, demonstrating Van Gogh's interest in creating unified decorative series.

## Stylistic Significance

- **Paris-period transformation.** The painting documents the crucial moment when Van Gogh was absorbing Impressionist and Neo-Impressionist influences, transforming his art from dark realism to vibrant colour.
- **Suburban avant-garde.** The Asnières and Clichy area attracted multiple avant-garde artists in the 1880s, and Van Gogh's work from this suburb places him within that progressive artistic community.
- **Triptych format.** The red-bordered triptych demonstrates Van Gogh's early interest in creating groups of related paintings — a practice he would develop further with the Sunflower and flower decorations.
