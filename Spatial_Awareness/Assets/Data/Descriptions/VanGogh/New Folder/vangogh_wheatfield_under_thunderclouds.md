# Wheatfield under Thunderclouds

## Metadata

- **Title:** Wheatfield under Thunderclouds (Korenveld onder onweerslucht)
- **Artist:** Vincent van Gogh (1853–1890)
- **Year:** July 1890
- **Medium:** Oil on canvas
- **Dimensions:** 50.4 × 101.3 cm (19.8 × 39.9 in)
- **Catalogue raisonné:** F 778, JH 2097
- **Location:** Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam (Vincent van Gogh Foundation)
- **Provenance:** Sent to Theo van Gogh, Paris, after mid-July 1890; inherited by Jo van Gogh-Bonger and Vincent Willem van Gogh; Vincent van Gogh Foundation, 1962; Van Gogh Museum, 1973
- **Period:** Auvers-sur-Oise
- **Genre:** Landscape painting

## Subject

A vast expanse of green wheat field stretches across the entire width of an unusually elongated canvas, beneath a dark, heavily clouded sky. The composition is radically simple: two horizontal planes — green-gold wheat below, blue-grey sky above — divided by a low horizon line roughly at the centre. A few heavy white clouds press down against the deep blue sky. The wheat, not yet ripe, shimmers in shades of green and yellow-green. No trees, buildings, roads, or figures interrupt the immense panorama. The effect is of boundless, elemental nature — simultaneously beautiful and foreboding.

## Visual Description

- **Palette:** The lower half is dominated by greens and yellows — the living wheat field. The upper half is deep blue with heavy white and grey cloud forms. The contrast between the vivid green earth and the threatening dark sky creates a powerful emotional tension.
- **Brushwork:** Bold, horizontal strokes for the wheat, with broader, more agitated strokes for the sky. The technique is confident and economical — Van Gogh achieves his effect with sweeping gestures rather than detailed rendering.
- **Composition:** The double-square format (approximately 50 × 100 cm) is unique to Van Gogh's final Auvers landscapes. The elongated horizontal format dramatically emphasises the vastness and flatness of the landscape and the weight of the sky.
- **Atmosphere:** The dark, turbulent sky pressing down on the bright, living wheat creates a mood of sublime tension — beauty suffused with threat.

## Context

Wheatfield under Thunderclouds was painted in early July 1890, during Van Gogh's final weeks in Auvers-sur-Oise. It belongs to a series of approximately thirteen large horizontal canvases depicting the local wheat harvest, all in the unusual elongated double-square format. Van Gogh wrote to Theo around July 10, 1890: "They depict vast, distended wheatfields under angry skies, and I deliberately tried to express sadness and extreme loneliness in them."

Yet he also found solace in the overwhelming landscape: "I'd almost believe that these canvases will tell you what I can't say in words, what I consider healthy and fortifying about the countryside."

Van Gogh died on July 29, 1890 — approximately three weeks after completing this painting. By that date, the painting was already with Theo in Paris.

## Artistic Intent

- **Express sadness and extreme loneliness.** Van Gogh explicitly stated that the wheatfield series was intended to convey his deepest emotional states.
- **Find healing in nature.** Despite the melancholy, Van Gogh believed these landscapes communicated something "healthy and fortifying" about the countryside.
- **Push the boundaries of landscape format.** The extreme horizontal format was a deliberate experiment in emphasising vastness and simplicity.

## Stylistic Significance

- **Among Van Gogh's last paintings.** Created weeks before his death, the wheatfield series represents the culmination of his landscape art.
- **Radical simplification.** The extreme reduction of the composition to two horizontal zones anticipates the colour field painting of the twentieth century.
- **The double-square format.** The elongated canvases (including Wheatfield with Crows and Landscape at Auvers in the Rain) form a unique group in Van Gogh's oeuvre, exploring the relationship between panoramic format and emotional expression.
