# Landscape at Saint-Rémy (Enclosed Field with Peasant)

## Metadata

- **Title:** Landscape at Saint-Rémy (Enclosed Field with Peasant)
- **Artist:** Vincent van Gogh
- **Year:** 1889 (early October)
- **Medium:** Oil on canvas
- **Dimensions:** 73.66 × 92.07 cm (29 × 36 1/4 in)
- **Catalogue raisonné:** F641 / JH1795
- **Location:** Indianapolis Museum of Art at Newfields, Indianapolis, Indiana, USA
- **Credit line:** Gift of Mrs. James W. Fesler in memory of Daniel W. and Elizabeth C. Marmon, 1944
- **Period:** Saint-Rémy period (1889–1890)
- **Genre:** Landscape with figure

## Subject

A view of the walled wheat field visible from the asylum at Saint-Paul-de-Mausole, now ploughed and showing broken lilac-toned earth in mounded furrows. In the foreground, a small peasant figure carries a wheatsheaf, rendered in violet and yellow tones. A lilac bush and dry grass with thistles appear in a corner. In the middle ground, a low wall encloses the field, with several buildings visible beyond. The Alpilles mountains fill the entire background, rising to meet the sky and occupying the upper portion of the canvas. The mountains are rendered as undulating, rhythmic forms that seem to pulse with energy.

## Visual Description

- **Palette:** Dominated by lilacs, violets, and mauves of the ploughed earth and distant mountains, punctuated by ochres, yellows, and touches of green. The peasant figure is described by Van Gogh as "a little violet and yellow man." The overall tonality is cool and autumnal, with the broken earth suggesting recent harvest.
- **Brushwork:** Dynamic and heavily textured. The ploughed earth is rendered with thick, directional strokes following the furrows. The mountains use rhythmic, undulating strokes that create a powerful sense of geological movement. The surface is densely worked throughout, with rich impasto.
- **Composition:** The field occupies the lower half, with the mountains rising directly behind to fill the upper portion. There is no sky band — the mountains "go all the way up the canvas," as Van Gogh described. This compressed, almost airless composition amplifies the sense of enclosure.
- **Atmosphere:** Raw, elemental, and pantheistic. The land and mountains seem alive with internal energy, reflecting Van Gogh's spiritual identification with the forces of nature.

## Context

This painting is one of four views of the walled wheat field executed in autumn 1889, a field Van Gogh could see from the asylum grounds. The same field was the subject of his Wheat Field with Reaper (summer 1889). Van Gogh described it in two letters: to Émile Bernard as "a no. 30 canvas with broken lilac ploughed fields and a background of mountains that go all the way up the canvas; so nothing but rough ground and rocks, with a thistle and dry grass in a corner, and a little violet and yellow man"; and to Theo as "the same field as the one of the reaper. Now it's mounds of earth and the background parched lands, then the rocks of the Alpilles."

The painting was created en plein air over several days during one of the most tumultuous periods of Van Gogh's life. His physician permitted him to paint outside the asylum, understanding that working from nature eased his symptoms. The enclosed field, visible from the asylum but bounded by a wall, became a metaphor for Van Gogh's own confined yet still productive existence.

The painting was donated to the Indianapolis Museum of Art (now Newfields) in 1944 by Caroline Marmon Fesler and has been a cornerstone of the museum's collection since.

## Artistic Intent

- **Express pantheistic spirituality through landscape.** Van Gogh's intense identification with nature imbued even the most ordinary ploughed field with fertile inner life and spiritual resonance.
- **Create a seasonal counterpart to the Reaper.** Where the summer version showed wheat being harvested — which Van Gogh associated with death — this autumn version shows the aftermath: ploughed earth, a peasant carrying the last sheaf, the cycle continuing.
- **Compress space for emotional intensity.** By eliminating the sky and pushing the mountains to the top of the canvas, Van Gogh created a claustrophobic, enclosed composition that mirrors his own confinement.

## Stylistic Significance

- **Enclosed field series.** This painting belongs to a group of four autumn views of the same walled field, a series that tracks the seasonal transformation of the landscape and Van Gogh's evolving emotional state.
- **Skyless composition.** The elimination of sky in favour of mountains filling the entire upper canvas is unusual in Van Gogh's work and creates a particularly intense, earth-bound mood.
- **Thematic pairing with the Reaper.** Van Gogh explicitly connected this painting to his earlier Wheat Field with Reaper, making the two a complementary pair exploring the agricultural cycle as metaphor for life and death.
