# Vincent van Gogh — Fishing in Spring, the Pont de Clichy (Asnières)

## Metadata

- **Title:** Fishing in Spring, the Pont de Clichy (Asnières)
- **Original Dutch Title:** Vissen in de lente, de Pont de Clichy (Asnières)
- **Artist:** Vincent van Gogh
- **Year:** 1887
- **Medium:** Oil on canvas
- **Dimensions:** 50.5 × 60 cm (19 7/8 × 23 5/8 in)
- **Catalogue raisonné:** F354 / JH1270
- **Location:** Art Institute of Chicago, Chicago, USA
- **Credit line:** Gift of Charles Deering McCormick, Brooks McCormick, and Roger McCormick
- **Period:** Paris period (1886–1888)
- **Genre:** Landscape / Plein-air painting

## Subject

The painting depicts a solitary fisherman standing on the tree-lined bank of the Seine River near the Pont de Clichy bridge at Asnières, a suburb northwest of Paris. The figure wears blue pants, a yellow-brown shirt, and a black hat, and holds a fishing pole resting against his leg. Two green fishing boats are moored nearby along the bank. In the background, the stone Clichy bridge arches over the water, with buildings and lush trees visible on the far shore. The scene captures a quiet spring day along a stretch of the Seine frequently painted by Impressionist and Post-Impressionist artists.

## Visual Description

- **Palette:** Bright and varied — dominated by greens, blues, lavenders, and earth tones. A dramatic shift from the dark browns of Van Gogh's earlier Dutch-period works.
- **Brushwork:** Small, distinct strokes of pure color applied side by side, reflecting Neo-Impressionist (Pointillist/Divisionist) influence. Van Gogh combines pointillist-like dabs with his own developing style of thicker, directional dashes.
- **Water:** The Seine is rendered with lateral strokes ranging from cobalt to sky blue in the foreground, transitioning to lighter blues and white farther back. Reflections of the riverbank use multicolored vertical strokes — lavender, cobalt, greens, peach, and blue.
- **Vegetation:** The trees along the bank are painted in varied greens with visible individual brushstrokes, creating a vibrant, textured canopy. A prominent tree trunk rises in the left foreground.
- **Bridge:** The Pont de Clichy spans the middle-to-background, rendered in stone tones with a lavender and Prussian blue arch underneath.
- **Light:** Bright, diffused spring daylight. The overall atmosphere is luminous and tranquil.
- **Composition:** The fisherman is placed in the middle ground on the right bank. The river fills the center, with the bridge providing a horizontal anchor in the background. Foreground foliage and moored boats frame the scene.

## Context

Van Gogh painted this work during his two-year stay in Paris (March 1886 – February 1888), a transformative period in his artistic development. After arriving in Paris, he encountered the work of the Impressionists and Neo-Impressionists, particularly at the final Impressionist exhibition in spring 1886, where he saw paintings by Georges Seurat and Paul Signac.

Van Gogh and Signac became friends and frequently painted together along the Seine at Asnières and Clichy, suburbs on the northwestern outskirts of Paris that were popular painting locations among avant-garde artists. Signac introduced Van Gogh to the principles of Divisionism — the technique of applying small strokes of pure, unmixed color side by side to maximize optical luminosity.

## Artistic Intent

- **Experiment with Neo-Impressionist technique.** The painting demonstrates Van Gogh's active engagement with Divisionism and Pointillism, applying individual strokes of pure color to build luminosity. However, he never fully adopted the systematic, scientific approach of Seurat; his strokes remain more spontaneous and varied.
- **Brighten the palette.** This work is evidence of the radical shift from the dark earth tones of his Dutch period (e.g., The Potato Eaters, 1885) to the vivid, light-filled color he developed in Paris.
- **Capture plein-air atmosphere.** Van Gogh painted this scene outdoors, directly observing the interplay of spring light on water, foliage, and architecture.
- **Explore suburban landscape.** The Asnières/Clichy area represented a zone between city and countryside — a modern subject favored by Impressionist painters, where leisure activities like fishing coexisted with industrial-era infrastructure like bridges.

## Stylistic Significance

- **Transitional work.** Fishing in Spring sits between Van Gogh's dark Dutch realism and the bold, expressive style of his later Arles and Saint-Rémy periods. It documents the moment when his color sense was being fundamentally reshaped by Parisian avant-garde influences.
- **Relationship to Neo-Impressionism.** While Van Gogh absorbed Divisionist principles, he adapted them freely rather than following Seurat's methodical system. His brushstrokes are more varied in size and direction, and his application is more emotionally intuitive. This selective adoption would become characteristic of his mature style.
- **Connection to Signac.** The painting is a direct product of Van Gogh's friendship and painting excursions with Paul Signac. Their collaborative outdoor sessions along the Seine were formative for Van Gogh's evolving technique.

## Related Works

Van Gogh painted several other works at Asnières and along the Seine during this period, including: The Seine with the Pont de la Grande Jatte (1887), Bridges across the Seine at Asnières (1887), Restaurant de la Sirène at Asnières (1887), and Banks of the Seine (1887). Together, these works form a coherent group documenting his Paris-period experiments with color and light.
