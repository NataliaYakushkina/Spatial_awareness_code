# The Old Tower in the Fields

## Metadata

- **Title:** The Old Tower in the Fields (De oude toren 's avonds)
- **Artist:** Vincent van Gogh
- **Year:** 1884 (July)
- **Medium:** Oil on canvas on cardboard
- **Dimensions:** 35 × 47 cm (13.8 × 18.5 in)
- **Catalogue raisonné:** F40 / JH507
- **Location:** Private collection (sold at Sotheby's London, December 10, 1969)
- **Period:** Nuenen period (1883–1885) — Dutch period
- **Genre:** Landscape

## Subject

The painting depicts the old tower of the ruined medieval church in the village of Nuenen, seen across flat agricultural fields at dusk. A solitary figure wearing a white cap walks along a path leading toward the silhouetted tower, which rises prominently against the horizon. A pale moon is visible in the sky to the right of the tower. The scene is set in the evening, with muted twilight illumination.

## Visual Description

- **Palette:** Dark and muted — predominantly browns, greens, ochres, and grays. The sky blends muted greens and yellows. The overall tonality is somber and earthy, consistent with Van Gogh's pre-Paris Dutch palette.
- **Brushwork:** Broad, somewhat coarse strokes with a controlled but energetic quality. Less refined than his later work but already showing directional brushwork to convey texture in the field and sky.
- **Composition:** Horizontal format. The tower is positioned off-center, rising above a low, flat horizon line. The figure on the path provides scale and a focal point for the viewer's eye to follow toward the tower. The vast, empty field emphasizes the solitude of both figure and ruin.
- **Light:** Dim, diffused twilight. The moon provides a pale accent but does not illuminate the scene. The atmosphere is quiet and contemplative.

## Context

Van Gogh painted this work during his stay in Nuenen, a small village in the Dutch province of North Brabant, where he lived with his parents from December 1883 to November 1885. The old church tower in Nuenen was a recurring subject for Van Gogh; he painted and drew it multiple times in various states and seasons. The tower was part of a medieval church that had largely fallen into ruin. Van Gogh was drawn to the motif as a symbol of the passage of time and the persistence of the rural landscape.

This period was Van Gogh's formative Dutch phase, during which he focused on peasant life, rural landscapes, and dark-toned realism heavily influenced by the Barbizon School, Dutch Golden Age painting, and the work of Jean-François Millet.

## Artistic Intent

- **Document the rural Dutch landscape.** Van Gogh was committed to painting the countryside of Brabant as he observed it — plain, unembellished, and honest.
- **Convey atmosphere and mood.** The evening light, solitary figure, and ruined tower create a meditative, melancholic tone emphasizing solitude, transience, and endurance.
- **Study the motif of the old tower.** The Nuenen tower served as a repeated subject through which Van Gogh explored different times of day, seasons, and compositional approaches.

## Stylistic Significance

- **Early Dutch period.** This painting is representative of Van Gogh's pre-Paris style: dark palette, earthen tones, realist subject matter, and heavy atmospheric mood.
- **Sharp contrast with later work.** Comparing this painting to works from just three years later (such as Fishing in Spring or The Bedroom) illustrates the dramatic transformation of Van Gogh's palette and technique after his move to Paris.
- **Recurring motif.** Van Gogh painted the Nuenen tower in multiple works, including The Old Church Tower at Nuenen (F88) and The Cemetery of Nuenen in the Snow. Together, these form a small series documenting a single landmark across varying conditions.
