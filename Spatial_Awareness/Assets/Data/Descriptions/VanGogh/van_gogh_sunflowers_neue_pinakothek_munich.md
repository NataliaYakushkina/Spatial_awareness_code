# Sunflowers (Neue Pinakothek, Munich)

## Metadata

- **Title:** Sunflowers (Tournesols) — also known as Vase with Twelve Sunflowers
- **Artist:** Vincent van Gogh
- **Year:** 1888 (August)
- **Medium:** Oil on canvas
- **Dimensions:** 91 × 72 cm (35.8 × 28.3 in)
- **Catalogue raisonné:** F456 / JH1561
- **Location:** Neue Pinakothek, Munich, Germany
- **Period:** Arles period (1888–1889)
- **Genre:** Still life

## Subject

A bouquet of twelve sunflowers arranged in a yellow earthenware vase, set against a pale turquoise-blue background. The flowers are depicted at various stages — from tight buds to fully open radiant blooms to heavy, seed-laden heads beginning to droop. The vase sits on a flat surface with Van Gogh's signature visible on it. This is one of the first two large Arles Sunflowers paintings and was among those Van Gogh intended to decorate Gauguin's room in the Yellow House.

## Visual Description

- **Palette:** Dominated by yellows — chrome yellow, cadmium yellow, ochre — for the flowers and vase, set against a light blue-turquoise background. The contrast between warm yellow and cool blue creates a vibrant complementary tension. Green stems and leaves provide additional accents.
- **Brushwork:** Thick impasto, especially on the flower heads, where paint is built up in sculptural ridges. Petals are rendered with curving, directional strokes. Seed centers use clusters of small dots and dabs. The background is more thinly and evenly painted.
- **Composition:** Vertical format. The vase is centered in the lower portion, with flowers filling the upper two-thirds. The arrangement is naturalistic and slightly asymmetrical, with some flowers drooping left or right.
- **Texture:** Highly tactile surface. The impasto on the flower petals creates actual three-dimensional relief on the canvas.

## Context

This is one of the original two large Arles Sunflowers painted in August 1888 as part of Van Gogh's decorative scheme for the Yellow House. He intended the sunflower paintings to hang in Gauguin's guest room as a welcoming gesture. The Munich version is distinguished by its turquoise-blue background (other versions have yellow or royal blue backgrounds) and by the composition of twelve flower heads.

Van Gogh described his working process in letters to Theo, noting he was painting with the speed and energy of a Marseillais eating bouillabaisse. He worked on the paintings in a frenzy of productivity during the hot August days, completing several large canvases in rapid succession.

## Artistic Intent

- **Create a decorative ensemble for the Yellow House.** The Sunflowers were conceived as part of a larger interior decoration scheme, not as isolated easel paintings.
- **Explore chromatic range within a single hue.** By working primarily in yellows against a blue ground, Van Gogh tested how far he could push tonal variation within a restricted palette.
- **Symbolize gratitude and welcome.** The flowers were explicitly made for Gauguin's arrival and were later considered by Van Gogh as part of a triptych with La Berceuse symbolizing gratitude.
- **Capture all stages of the sunflower's life cycle.** Buds, blooms, and withered heads appear together, compressing growth and decay into a single arrangement.

## Stylistic Significance

- **One of the original Arles pair.** The Munich version is among the first two Arles Sunflowers and thus part of the foundational series that Van Gogh later replicated. It predates the repetitions made at Saint-Rémy.
- **Blue-background variant.** The turquoise-blue background distinguishes this version visually from the more commonly reproduced yellow-background versions and provides a stronger complementary contrast.
- **Global icon.** The Arles Sunflowers collectively are among the most recognized and reproduced paintings in Western art history, and the Munich version is one of the most important surviving examples.
