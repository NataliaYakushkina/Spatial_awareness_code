# The Bedroom

## Metadata

- **Title:** The Bedroom (La Chambre à coucher)
- **Artist:** Vincent van Gogh
- **Year:** 1888 (first version)
- **Medium:** Oil on canvas
- **Dimensions:** 72 × 90 cm (28.3 × 35.4 in)
- **Location:** Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam, Netherlands (first version)
- **Period:** Arles period (1888–1889)
- **Genre:** Interior / Genre painting

## Subject

The painting depicts Van Gogh's own bedroom at 2 Place Lamartine in Arles, France, in the building known as the Yellow House. The room contains a bed with a red coverlet and yellow-green pillows, two rush-seated chairs, a small table with a water jug and basin, a mirror, a towel on a hook, and paintings hanging on the walls. A window with green shutters is visible on the far wall. Two doors lead off the room: the right door opens onto the staircase and upper floor, and the left door leads to the guest room Van Gogh prepared for Paul Gauguin. The room is trapezoidal rather than rectangular, which contributes to the skewed perspective.

## Visual Description

- **Palette:** Bold flat areas of contrasting color. Walls are pale lilac-violet (originally more distinctly purple, now faded due to pigment degradation of red cochineal lake). Floor is red tiles (also shifted toward blue over time). Bed frame and chairs are fresh butter yellow. Pillows and sheet are lemon green. Blanket is scarlet red. Window is green. Table is orange. Basin is blue. Doors are lilac.
- **Brushwork:** Relatively flat and uniform compared to Van Gogh's other works. Broad areas of single color with visible but controlled strokes. Outlines are distinct, almost cartoon-like.
- **Perspective:** Deliberately distorted. The floor tilts sharply forward, walls converge at unusual angles, and furniture appears to lean. The bed is disproportionately large relative to other objects. This is intentional, not a draftsmanship error.
- **Composition:** Symmetrical in spirit — bed centered against the back wall, flanked by chairs, with paired paintings above. The closed shutters and absence of figures create an enclosed, intimate space.
- **Light:** No visible light source. The room is evenly illuminated with no shadows, contributing to a flat, dreamlike quality.

## Context

Van Gogh painted the first version of The Bedroom in October 1888, approximately one month after moving into the Yellow House in Arles. This was the first home he had ever had of his own, and he was enthusiastically decorating it with paintings in preparation for Gauguin's arrival. Exhausted from decorating, he spent two and a half days bedridden, during which he conceived the painting.

He wrote to his brother Theo that the color alone should carry the meaning, and that looking at the painting should rest the mind and suggest sleep. He made three versions: the original 1888 painting (Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam), a same-scale copy painted in September 1889 at the Saint-Rémy asylum (Art Institute of Chicago), and a smaller version made as a gift for his mother and sister (Musée d'Orsay, Paris). The three versions differ primarily in the small paintings depicted on the wall.

## Artistic Intent

- **Express restfulness through color alone.** Van Gogh explicitly stated that the simplified color scheme — complementary pairs of yellow/purple and green/red — should evoke sleep and repose without narrative content.
- **Achieve simplicity and solidity.** He wanted the sturdy furniture and bare surfaces to convey stability and permanence, in contrast to his turbulent inner life.
- **Demonstrate that color can replace modeling and shadow.** By eliminating cast shadows and using flat areas of pure color, he explored a proto-Fauvist approach where hue and contrast do the emotional work traditionally assigned to light and shade.
- **Create a personal sanctuary.** The painting is an idealized portrait of domestic peace — a life Van Gogh longed for but struggled to maintain.

## Stylistic Significance

- **Japanese print influence.** The flat color fields, bold outlines, and absence of shadow reflect Van Gogh's deep admiration for Japanese ukiyo-e woodblock prints, which he collected and studied extensively.
- **Precursor to Fauvism and Expressionism.** The use of arbitrary, emotionally coded color rather than observed local color anticipates developments by Matisse, Derain, and the German Expressionists.
- **Color degradation.** Scientific analysis has revealed significant pigment fading. The walls were originally a distinct violet (cobalt blue mixed with carmine cochineal lake), and the floor was redder. The painting's current appearance differs substantially from Van Gogh's original intent.
- **Personal iconography.** The three versions together form a rare case of Van Gogh deliberately replicating his own work, demonstrating how much this particular image meant to him.
