# Self-Portrait with Straw Hat

## Metadata

- **Title:** Self-Portrait with Straw Hat
- **Artist:** Vincent van Gogh
- **Year:** 1887 (summer, July–August)
- **Medium:** Oil on canvas (painted on the reverse of The Potato Peeler, an earlier peasant study)
- **Dimensions:** 41.8 × 31.5 cm (16.5 × 12.4 in)
- **Catalogue raisonné:** F469 (Van Gogh Museum version); F365v (Metropolitan Museum version, also on reverse of another painting)
- **Location:** Multiple versions exist. The most widely reproduced version is at the Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam. Another notable version is at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York.
- **Period:** Paris period (1886–1888)
- **Genre:** Self-portrait

## Subject

A bust-length self-portrait of Van Gogh wearing a yellow straw hat and a blue work coat or smock. He faces slightly to the right (his mirror-reversed left), looking directly at the viewer with an intense, penetrating gaze. His red beard is visible beneath the hat brim. The background is rendered in swirling, directional brushstrokes of mixed colors.

## Visual Description

- **Palette:** Bright and varied. The straw hat is rendered in yellows and golds. The coat is blue with green undertones. The face uses warm flesh tones with green and blue shadows. The background mixes blues, greens, yellows, and oranges in short directional strokes. The overall effect is vibrant and colorful.
- **Brushwork:** Small, distinct, directional strokes applied in a Neo-Impressionist manner. Different areas of the composition use different stroke directions — radiating outward from the face in the background, following the contours of the hat, and modeling the features of the face. The technique is looser and more spontaneous than strict Pointillism.
- **Color discoloration:** The painting has suffered significant pigment degradation over time. A purple priming layer has largely faded, and what was originally a purple background (intended to contrast with the yellow hat) has shifted considerably. The current appearance does not fully represent Van Gogh's original color scheme.
- **Expression:** The eyes are notably intense — one appears blue, the other greenish. The mouth is tight, the face gaunt. The overall expression is one of focused self-examination.

## Context

Van Gogh painted over 25 self-portraits during his two years in Paris (1886–1888), primarily because he could not afford to hire models. Self-portraiture served as both practical exercise and a means of artistic self-definition. The straw hat and work clothes were deliberately chosen to present himself as a working man's artist, aligned with peasant laborers rather than bourgeois society.

This painting was executed during the summer of 1887, at the height of Van Gogh's engagement with Neo-Impressionist technique. His friendships with Signac, Bernard, and Toulouse-Lautrec were shaping his evolving approach to color and brushwork. Van Gogh reused canvases and cardboard to save money, which is why this self-portrait was painted on the reverse of an earlier peasant study.

## Artistic Intent

- **Practice figure painting and self-examination.** Self-portraits allowed Van Gogh to study facial structure, expression, and color in flesh tones without the expense of a model.
- **Experiment with Neo-Impressionist color.** The pointillist-inflected brushwork and complementary color contrasts (yellow/purple, blue/orange) reflect his active absorption of Divisionist principles.
- **Project artistic identity.** The straw hat and work clothes are not casual choices — they position Van Gogh as a painter of the people, distinct from the salon artists of Paris.

## Stylistic Significance

- **Paris period experimentation.** The painting documents the pivotal transitional phase when Van Gogh was absorbing Impressionist and Neo-Impressionist influences and radically transforming his palette.
- **Pigment degradation as art-historical problem.** The severe fading of red pigments has fundamentally altered the painting's appearance, making it an important case study in conservation science and the impermanence of certain 19th-century pigments.
- **Series within the oeuvre.** This is one of a cluster of straw-hat self-portraits from summer 1887, each exploring slightly different compositional and chromatic approaches to the same basic motif.
